1 — Associate POS Interface

Touch-first transaction surface designed for the counter: high contrast for bright store lighting, large tap targets, minimal cognitive load under queue pressure. Three states tell the operational story: idle (shift ready), mid-transaction (building a cart), and payment (fiscal receipt issued). The associate authenticates with a badge or PIN, scans products or selects them visually, and completes payment via split tender when needed.

Design Notes

  • High contrast dark theme for store lighting — retail floors are brightly lit; a light UI on a counter tablet washes out. Dark chrome + bright product tiles reduce glare.
  • Four primary actions are the whole mental model — Scan · Customer · Promo · Returns. Everything else is secondary. No menus to navigate during a transaction.
  • Frequently-sold surfaces first — the store's top SKUs appear as tap-to-add tiles. Associates who can't find a barcode can still ring up quickly.
  • Stock visibility inline — "● 12 in stock" / "● Low · 2 left" appears on every tile. Prevents over-promise. The green/amber dot is reusable with low cognitive load.
  • Scan-focus keyboard shortcut (F1) — for stores with barcode guns, the scanner input must never lose focus. Named explicitly so associates trained on other POS systems feel at home.
  • Shift context in the right panel — float, transactions, auto-close time. Visible without intruding on the cart area.

Design Notes

  • Customer panel is the unified-commerce payoff — "4th visit this store · also shops online" and the loyalty tier come from the unified customer record that online shares with in-store. Associates see the whole relationship, not just today's visit.
  • Cross-channel signal card — an AI-tinted card surfaces the online wishlist in the physical moment. "In stock here" closes the online-to-offline loop. One tap to Add to cart, no typing.
  • Frequently-paired products react to the cart — "frequently-paired with cart items" retitles the lower grid once items are added. Helps associates upsell without memorizing pairings.
  • Item meta shows tax per line — for markets where VAT or sales tax is itemized on receipts, per-item tax is visible during the transaction. Reduces receipt confusion.

  • Tap targets are 28px minimum — qty −/+ buttons scale to finger-first interaction. Keyboard shortcuts available for associates who prefer them.

Design Notes

  • Fiscal receipt is provably issued, not just promised — the vendor device signature appears in the confirmation. GB-HMRC compliant callout names the country regime. This is the partnership-accelerated promise made visible in-UX.
  • Split tender is first-class, not a workaround — three lines (card + gift card + cash) is a common real-world pattern. Shown directly, not hidden behind a "more payment methods" overflow.
  • Receipt delivery is a choice, not a default — Print / Email / SMS as three large buttons. Email is pre-filled with the customer's email (if identified). GDPR-friendly: no data sent without explicit action.
  • Loyalty feedback is immediate — "+291 points" and "1,289 to Platinum" told at the moment of payment, not deferred to a next-visit email. Encourages repeat purchase narrative at the counter.
  • "Print & new sale" is a distinct path — busy counters often print and rush. A shortcut that auto-advances to the next transaction respects the operational reality.
  • Transaction ID format is traceable — TXN-2026050713201 embeds date + till + sequence. Associates can read it back for exchange/returns without system lookup.

2 — Unified Customer & Inventory

The structural promise of unified commerce is that online and in-store converge into one customer record and one inventory pool. Store Managers need to see that convergence to operate against it — a shopper's full online+offline history, store-level performance relative to e-commerce, and inventory visibility across channels (click-and-collect, ship-from-store, BOPIS returns). This tab designs the Store Manager's primary working surfaces.

Design Notes

  • Timeline interleaves channels — today's in-store purchase sits above last week's online wishlist addition, above last month's ship-from-store order. The channel badge is prefixed, but time is the primary axis.
  • Loyalty events are first-class — "Promoted to Gold tier" is a distinct event type (violet accent) so managers see lifecycle milestones without hunting.
  • Sidebar facts establish the relationship shape — "Home store: Regent St." + "Prefers: In-store pickup" + "Size profile" are the three facts that let a store manager prepare for a visit.

  • Appointment notes inline — styling appointment notes ("prefers minimalist palette") give the next associate an immediate advantage without requiring them to dig.
  • Points earned per transaction — visible on every purchase event. Makes the loyalty math auditable at a glance.

Design Notes

  • This store is one node on the network, not an island — "This store" and "Network" columns side by side make the relationship obvious. Store managers see that a low stock here doesn't mean a lost sale elsewhere.
  • Wishlist signals drive retail decisions — "Leather Weekender Bag · wishlist hotspot · 8 wishlists online" with a "Promote in-store" action connects online intent to offline merchandising.

3 — Fiscal Compliance & Vendor Integration

The partnership-accelerated approach makes a clear boundary: the platform owns commerce, the vendor owns fiscal compliance. EU fiscal printer regulations across 20+ country regimes (Italy's RT registers, Germany's TSE, Poland's KSeF, France's NF-525, etc.) are impossible to own in-house without years of specialization. The vendor integration surface gives IT Admins visibility into per-store certification status, vendor SLA health, and the audit trail that regulators demand — without forcing the platform team to become fiscal-law experts.

Design Notes

  • Country regime is named, not buried — "HMRC MTD" / "TSE (KassenSichV)" / "NF-525" etc. IT Admins don't need to know the regulations, but seeing the regime name + cert number proves the platform knows.
  • Monospaced cert numbers — these are regulator-auditable identifiers. Copyable, unambiguous, same format everywhere.
  • Cert expiring alert with 30-day window — the amber row for Prenzlauer is visible in the sort order, not hidden in a filter. "23d" remaining is the decision-relevant number.
  • Device offline ≠ cert expired — Via Roma's offline device is a different failure mode. Till has switched to cash-only (vendor-dictated fallback) and a support ticket was opened automatically. The store keeps operating within fiscal law, at reduced capacity.
  • Vendor ticket number surfaced, not hidden behind a link — #FP-88421 is the contract of the partnership: IT Admin never opens a ticket manually for fiscal issues; the integration does it for them.
  • Export audit pack — one button to generate the evidence regulators ask for. Cert PDFs + audit log + device signatures bundled.

Integration boundary · who owns what

🟦 commercetools Platform owns
  • · Commerce logic (cart, pricing, promotions, loyalty)
  • · Unified customer & inventory
  • · POS UI + associate workflows
  • · Receipt content + delivery (email/SMS/print)
  • · Audit log of platform events
  • · Integration APIs to vendor device
🤝 Vendor (FiscalPrintersGmbH) owns
  • · Country regime certifications (20+ regimes)
  • · Fiscal device hardware + firmware
  • · Signed fiscal receipts (regulator-grade)
  • · Credit note issuance for returns
  • · Cert rotation, audit filings, regulatory updates
  • · Hardware support + on-site response

Design Notes

  • Integration boundary made explicit as a split panel — "Platform owns" vs "Vendor owns" side by side. Every conversation with an auditor or a frustrated store manager can reference this boundary instead of re-explaining.
  • SLA visible as a headline promise + current state — contract SLA (99.8%) against actual 30d uptime (99.94%). Over-performing looks different from meeting the minimum.
  • Regime coverage names what's available — ES and PT shown as "available · not yet deployed." When the retailer expands to Spain, adding fiscal support is store provisioning, not a six-month engineering project.
  • "20+ country regimes available under partnership" — stated plainly as the headline value of the partnership. This one sentence is the argument against building fiscal compliance in-house.
  • Vendor tickets embedded, not external-only — IT Admins see ticket status without leaving the console. Deep-link to the vendor portal is available but not required.
  • "Renew contract" as a first-class button — surfaced 243 days before expiry. Contract management is visible, not discovered through procurement emails a month before renewal.
The POS frame uses a distinct dark-mode high-contrast treatment appropriate to store-floor lighting and tap-first interaction. Admin surfaces (Tabs 2 and 3) inherit commercetools Merchant Center design tokens for consistency with the rest of the platform.
