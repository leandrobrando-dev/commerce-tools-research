02

The seven-moat matrix — dimension by dimension

Each row is one moat Epic 8 defends. Scoring is honest — we credit Amplitude+Statsig for real capabilities and mark partial coverage where it exists. Every verdict is anchored in a structural reason, not a marketing argument: commerce context cannot be reached without the commerce data model; Two-Horizon cannot be computed without CLV modeling on commerce outcomes; Green/Red Zone governance cannot be enforced without owning the runtime. Those aren't roadmap gaps — they are product-architecture gaps.

1
Commerce context
CLV tier, contract price, catalog segment, B2B approval queue state, account tier — read natively at the component runtime, not via webhook.
FR21–28 · FR52–64
Structural gap
Amplitude ingests SDK events. Statsig ingests flag exposures. Neither has native access to commercetools' catalog, pricing service, or approval workflows. Closing this requires acquiring a commerce backend — the only credible path is M&A, and no comparable platform is acquirable at their current valuation.
Native
Epic 8 reads commerce context at the point of render through the commercetools data model. A recommendation knows that this session is a mid-tier CLV consumer on the summer catalog; or that it's a B2B buyer whose account is at $4,900 of a $5,000 approval threshold. No webhook, no attribution stitching — the platform is the storefront.
2
Two-Horizon Measurement
H1 (CTR, CVR in 3-5 days) and H2 (CLV delta, repeat purchase, AOV over 90 days) gated independently and measured in parallel.
FR54 · FR55
Partial — separate products
Amplitude has retention analytics. Statsig has experimentation with CUPED and sequential testing. Neither has an opinionated commerce-specific H1/H2 model where recommendations surface only after clearing horizon-specific signal gates. Combining them would require a custom pipeline that no Amplitude/Statsig customer runs today.
Opinionated & first-class
Every Epic 8 experiment measures H1 and H2 in parallel, in the same pipeline. Recommendations with strong H1 but weak H2 (the "selection effect" failure mode — short-term wins that hurt CLV) are caught automatically and feed the Tried-and-Retired library. This is the mechanism that prevents optimising for the wrong outcome.
3
Green / Red Zone governance
AI composes freely from the component library (Green Zone); pricing, checkout, auth, approvals are immutable (Red Zone) — hard boundary enforced at a single runtime chokepoint.
FR13-14 · FR15 · FR33
No structural equivalent
Statsig has feature flags + targeted rollouts + permissions. Flags toggle behaviour; they cannot structurally prevent AI from proposing a change to pricing logic — governance depends on a policy layer and human review. Different architecture: flags filter, Green/Red Zone forbids.
Architectural, not policy
One file to audit: executor.ts. AI operates exclusively within the Green Zone. Red Zone is not a permission — it is a hard boundary the runtime enforces. An auditor can trace every AI mutation back to a single enforcement point. This is the enterprise-procurement answer to "how do we trust AI with our checkout flow?"
4
Auto-generated GitHub PRs for beyond-Green-Zone changes
When a recommendation needs new component logic, the engine produces a full code diff + schema update + behavioural evidence + preview deployment.
FR58
Out of scope
Statsig feature flags gate existing behaviour — they produce no code artefact. Amplitude+Statsig cannot write a schema update to a component library it does not own. This entire category of work is invisible to a horizontal experimentation platform.
Platform-native
AI writes the full PR: code diff, schema migration, behavioural evidence brief, preview deploy. Developer reviews and merges, or doesn't. Recommendations that require code changes stop being blocked by engineering capacity — they become tracked, reviewable, commerce-contextual pull requests. This is an order of magnitude more ambitious than feature flags.
5
Progressive rollout tied to the component library
5 → 25 → 50 → 100% gates run against governed component versions, per-tenant hot-reload, atomic rollback of the commerce-governed runtime.
FR57 · FR59
Same vocabulary, different scope
Statsig does progressive rollouts on feature flags at enterprise grade. But their rollouts gate toggles; ours gate governed components with commerce state. A Statsig rollout cannot atomically revert a pricing rule change or a checkout flow variant — those live outside its model.
Component-scoped
Same gate cadence, different unit of control. A progressive rollout in Epic 8 advances the version of a governed component through tenant storefronts with the commerce context intact — CLV tier-aware cohorts, B2B account-aware exclusions, atomic rollback of the full runtime state. The component library is the unit of deployment, not a flag.
6
Tenant Intelligence Score
0–100 composite metric compounding monthly from commerce-specific signals: experiment history, CLV cohort depth, prediction accuracy on commerce outcomes.
FR61
Cannot compute — no commerce model
Even with every session event and every flag exposure, Amplitude+Statsig cannot weight experiment outcomes by CLV tier or segment by contract-pricing context — neither data is reachable. A "tenant intelligence" metric without commerce context is just engagement data; it does not compound the moat.
The moat's scoreboard
Every experiment, CLV cohort rotation, and prediction-vs-outcome delta feeds the score. It is the most honest answer to "why not migrate to a competitor?" — because migrating drops the score to zero and rebuilds the model from scratch. The score is the visible manifestation of the compounding moat.
7
First-party, cookieless, GDPR-native ingestion
Behavioral data collected at the storefront where commerce happens — no SDK instrumentation, no cookie barriers, no third-party tracking, no attribution stitching.
FR21-28 · FR47-49
Still SDK + cookie dependent
Amplitude captures events via SDK; Statsig via flag exposure. In a world where 67% of US users block or reject tracking cookies, that means external tools see a fraction of actual traffic. GDPR compliance is possible but adds friction; enterprise customers carry the privacy risk.
Platform-native — +40-50% data capture
Behavioral data never leaves the platform — consent barriers disappear when the collector is the renderer. Epic 8 captures 40-50% more behavioural signal than cookie-based external tools. Erasure, retention, and residency are enforced at the platform tenancy boundary, not bolted on.

Horizontal vs vertical consolidation — we play different games

Amplitude+Statsig+OpenAI is a textbook horizontal consolidation — unifying session analytics, experimentation, feature flags, and AI builder tooling across every software vertical. Epic 8 is a vertical consolidation — unifying frontend, editor, governance, experimentation, and intelligence inside one commerce platform. The two strategies look adjacent but compete for different budget lines and different buyers. This is the clearest single frame for "why not just use Amplitude?"

Horizontal play (them): Amplitude's partnership with Statsig and Raji's OpenAI connection together push Amplitude toward the top-left quadrant — a full platform for any software vertical, with premium access to OpenAI model capabilities. Their dot is intentionally not in the commerce-native quadrant. The OpenAI pitch is "evaluate and iterate on shipped code, for every AI builder."

Vertical play (us): Epic 8 sits in the top-right quadrant because the value is specifically commerce-contextual — CLV tier, contract price, catalog segment, approval state, Green/Red Zone. We are a full platform, but only for commerce. This is not a gap to close; it is the positioning.

Both positions can win. The question for the prospect is whether they want a commerce-blind horizontal consolidator or a commerce-contextual vertical consolidator. That frame is the single cleanest way to answer "why not Amplitude?" — because the answer is "you may well use them too, for your non-commerce stack. For the commerce frontend, you need something that speaks commerce."

05

Sales responses — prepared answers to the five questions you will hear

Every discovery call after May 5, 2026 will raise one of these five questions. These are the prepared, honest answers — no straw-men, no hand-waving. Each assumes the prospect is technically literate and already running one or more of Amplitude, Statsig, or Optimizely. None of them frame the competitor as inferior overall — all of them frame the positioning as different, with a specific reason to pick Epic 8 for the commerce frontend.

Why not just use Amplitude+Statsig? Isn't that enough?
For most of your non-commerce stack, it may well be enough. Amplitude is a strong event-analytics platform, and Statsig brings credible experimentation — the combined bundle will serve your product-analytics and feature-flagging needs. For the commerce frontend specifically, it is not enough. The combined platform reads SDK events and flag exposures, neither of which carries CLV tier, contract price, catalog segment, or approval-queue state. That commerce context is what makes a recommendation actionable in the first place. Our engine is built inside the commerce platform where that context lives natively. You may keep Amplitude for your marketing and product analytics across every vertical; our scope is the commerce frontend specifically.
We already pay $400K a year for Amplitude. Why add another tool?
You are not adding a tool — you are consolidating the stack that sits beside Amplitude. The average commercetools enterprise already pays $350K-$1M across Amplitude, ContentSquare, FullStory, and Optimizely for the commerce frontend. Epic 8's core platform is bundled into your existing commercetools subscription — no incremental line item. The AI Experience Engine is consumption-based, with the first 10 experiments per tenant free. If you keep Amplitude for your non-commerce stack and use Epic 8 for the commerce frontend, you still eliminate ContentSquare, FullStory, and Optimizely — that's a material TCO reduction in year one.
How are you different from Statsig's experimentation?
Statsig is a best-in-class horizontal experimentation platform. We are a vertical experimentation platform, and the vertical difference matters in three specific places. First, Two-Horizon Measurement — our engine measures H1 (CTR, CVR in days) and H2 (CLV, repeat purchase, AOV over 90 days) in the same pipeline. An experiment with great H1 but declining H2 — the "selection effect" failure — is caught automatically. Statsig can be configured to do this with effort; ours is the default. Second, Green/Red Zone — our runtime structurally prevents AI from proposing changes to pricing, checkout, or approval logic. That is enterprise-procurement risk mitigation Statsig's flag model cannot match. Third, progressive rollouts gate governed components, not just feature toggles — atomic rollback of a rendered storefront including its commerce state.
What happens if Amplitude adds commerce context in their roadmap?
They can't — not without acquiring a commerce backend. Commerce context (CLV tier, contract price, catalog segment, approval state) is not a data integration problem; it's a data-ownership problem. Amplitude reads events via webhook or SDK. To weight an experiment by CLV tier, they would need continuous, low-latency access to the full commercetools customer-group + order + pricing model. That is a multi-year integration for a customer-by-customer setup and still doesn't give them runtime-level Green/Red Zone governance. The only structural closer of this gap is M&A — if Amplitude acquires Shopify, BigCommerce, SAP commerce, Salesforce Commerce Cloud, or commercetools itself, we revisit ADR-002 that same week. Until then, the commerce-context gap is architectural, not a roadmap item.
We're already on Statsig. What do we do at renewal?
This is genuinely the right time to evaluate alternatives. The Statsig platform team is now at OpenAI; the brand, customers, and maintenance responsibility have transitioned to Amplitude. Pricing at renewal is an open question — Amplitude has not publicly disclosed how Statsig contracts will reprice or whether the warehouse-native architecture will be preserved long-term. If you are on commercetools, a commerce-native alternative is worth a 90-day evaluation: keep your existing Statsig contract live, stand up Epic 8 on a pilot storefront (first 10 experiments are free), and compare H1+H2 outcomes with CLV-tier-aware segmentation vs. your current Statsig setup. The renewal decision then gets made on evidence, not on vendor-transition uncertainty.

Revisit triggers — when this comparison becomes stale

A competitive document ages fast in a live consolidation. These are the triggers that invalidate specific sections or require a full rewrite. Scheduled revisit is 2026-11-08 (6 months). Any of the below occurs sooner, this doc gets updated within the same week and ADR-002 is revisited.

High-impact
Amplitude acquires a commerce backend (Shopify, BigCommerce, SAP, Salesforce Commerce Cloud, commercetools itself).
Impact: moats 1 & 7 collapse immediately; moats 2, 3, 5, 6 weakened. Full positioning rewrite required.

OpenAI launches a commerce-agent product with native storefront integration.
Impact: shifts Epic 8 positioning from "better commerce analytics" to "commerce-governed orchestration layer beneath the agents." Roadmap additions around agent interoperability needed.
High-impact
commercetools itself acquires a horizontal experimentation vendor.
Impact: potential re-scoping of Epic 8. ADR-002 and this comparison must be re-evaluated against the new in-house capability.
Medium-impact
Amplitude+Statsig ships a CLV-tier-aware or commerce-context-aware feature.
Impact: suggests investment in closing moat 1 without a backend acquisition. Evaluate the depth; most likely still a webhook-dependent partial fix.
Medium-impact
A commercetools customer adopts Amplitude+Statsig on top of Epic 8.
Impact: indicates the commerce-native pitch is not neutralizing theirs on the specific buyer. Case study required; messaging sharpening in the sales motion.
Medium-impact
Statsig customer churn pattern stabilises under Amplitude (positive retention signal).
Impact: reduces the renewal-window opportunity; shift GTM emphasis back to consolidation-pricing argument.
