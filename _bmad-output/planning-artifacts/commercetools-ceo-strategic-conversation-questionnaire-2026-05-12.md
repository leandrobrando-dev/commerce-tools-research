# Strategic Conversation Questionnaire — commercetools CEO

## 0 · Pre-Conversation Framing

### Why we're talking

We are building a commercetools-native AI storefront platform — operator-driven authoring (AI Site Builder + Migrator) + behavioral analytics + experimentation + autonomous optimization, packaged as an MC Custom Application. Our recent market research surfaced that commercetools' own 2025 launches — **Agentic Jumpstart, AgenticLift, Cora AI** — target the agentic-commerce surface (consumers + AI agents discovering and buying), not operator-driven storefront authoring. That non-collision finding is good news, but the conversation we want to have is about **whether our direction is complementary, competitive, or acquisition-relevant from commercetools' point of view.**

### Hypotheses we want to test

1. **commercetools sees its AI strategy as agent-discovery + checkout, not operator authoring.** ← if true, we are complementary; joint-pitch motion is open.
2. **commercetools' frontend strategy remains "delegate to SI partners + ISVs."** ← if true, our channel motion (Accenture/Song, EPAM, Orium, Valtech) is on-strategy.
3. **commercetools sees Adobe Commerce + RT-CDP and Salesforce Commerce Cloud + Data Cloud as the existential competitive frame** — not standalone CDPs or standalone storefront builders.
4. **commercetools' M&A philosophy is "partner deep, acquire rarely"** — our acquisition relevance is low today but rises if we land 5+ commercetools-paying lighthouse customers.

### Pre-conversation prep

Before the meeting, confirm the following and adjust questions accordingly:

- Current CEO and tenure (verify against [commercetools press](https://commercetools.com/about-us)) — Dirk Hoerig is the founder and was CEO; if the role has transitioned, the 5-year-vision questions should be tuned to the current incumbent's tenure
- Most recent earnings or funding milestone (private company, but partnership announcements and customer count are public signals)
- Any recent public statements on AI, frontends, or partnerships within the last 90 days
- Any competitive announcements in the last 90 days (Adobe, Salesforce, Bloomreach, Sitecore product launches relevant to composable commerce)

### Conversation tone guidance

Strategic, peer-level. **Do not pitch the product in this conversation.** Lead with curiosity about commercetools' direction; show evidence we've done our homework (Agentic Jumpstart launch, Cora AI, MC Custom App architecture). Save the partnership / acquisition framing for the back half of the conversation, only if natural openings appear. **The goal is to learn their thesis, not sell ours.**

## 1 · Strategic Frame — Composable Commerce Category Leadership

### Section purpose

Test whether commercetools sees the composable thesis as durable — or whether the CEO is privately worried about Adobe/Salesforce suite consolidation. This shapes whether we sit *with* commercetools against the suites, or whether we hedge.

### Questions

1. **Looking three years out, where do you see the composable-commerce category heading? Do you see further category expansion, or does the suite-vs-composable battle resolve toward one side?**
   - *Hypothesis being tested:* commercetools is positioning composable commerce as durable and growing.
   - *What we learn:* the implicit answer to "should we plan for a composable-friendly buyer for the next 5 years, or hedge with multi-platform support?"

2. **Adobe RT-CDP + Adobe Commerce, Salesforce Data Cloud + Commerce Cloud — these are increasingly bundled as single procurements. Where do you see commercetools' best-of-breed positioning landing in that frame?**
   - *Hypothesis being tested:* commercetools sees suite vendors as the primary competitive frame, with category-specific best-of-breed (commercetools, Bloomreach, Algolia, etc.) as the alternative procurement model.
   - *What we learn:* whether their narrative is "we beat Adobe Commerce on TCO + agility" or something else.

3. **The CDP category itself is splintering — analyst-leader CDPs (Adobe, Salesforce, Tealium) bundled in suites; warehouse-first CDPs (RudderStack, Snowplow, Hightouch) standalone. Does commercetools have a stated position on which CDP architecture fits best with composable commerce?**
   - *Hypothesis being tested:* commercetools has a soft preference for warehouse-first / dev-led CDPs (consistent with composable thesis) but no formal partnership stance.
   - *What we learn:* whether there's a partnership lane open with the warehouse-first CDPs or whether commercetools is committed elsewhere.

## 2 · AI & Agentic Commerce Thesis

### Section purpose

Validate that Agentic Jumpstart is the agent-discovery layer, not the storefront-authoring layer. Probe how far "downward" their AI scope extends.

### Questions

4. **Agentic Jumpstart launched November 2025 with AI Hub + Agent Gateway, supporting MCP / A2A / AP2 / ACP. What's the 18-month vision after launch — do you go deeper into AI agent discovery + checkout, or extend the AI scope to other parts of the commerce stack?**
   - *Hypothesis being tested:* the next 18 months stay focused on agent-discovery + checkout (deeper, not wider).
   - *What we learn:* whether storefront authoring is in or out of their AI roadmap.

5. **Cora AI is described as an AI assistant with Vertex AI–powered Intelligent Search. How do you see Cora's scope evolving — is it primarily a Merchant Center co-pilot, a customer-facing assistant, or both?**
   - *Hypothesis being tested:* Cora is operator-facing inside Merchant Center, not a storefront-authoring assistant.
   - *What we learn:* whether commercetools is building anything that overlaps with our AI Site Builder Create-mode.

6. **AgenticLift was announced at NRF 2026 as a "standalone agentic offering." How does it differ from Agentic Jumpstart, and what customer problem does it solve that the original couldn't?**
   - *Hypothesis being tested:* AgenticLift is a packaging variant for smaller customers, not a new product surface.
   - *What we learn:* the breadth of commercetools' agentic-commerce SKU strategy.

7. **Where does commercetools' AI investment stop? Are there specific categories — operator UI, storefront authoring, behavioral analytics, experimentation — that you've consciously decided to leave to partners and ISVs?**
   - *Hypothesis being tested:* yes — frontend authoring + experimentation are explicitly partner-territory in their internal roadmap.
   - *What we learn:* this is the **single most important question in the entire conversation.** Direct answer either green-lights our positioning or surfaces a roadmap collision.

## 3 · Partnership & Ecosystem Strategy

### Section purpose

Test whether the SI-partner motion (Accenture/Song, EPAM, Orium, Valtech) and the ISV motion are equally weighted, or whether one is dominant. Probe channel architecture for our product.

### Questions

8. **Agentic Jumpstart's launch SI partners — Accenture (Song), EPAM, Orium, Valtech — represent significant channel investment. How is the SI relationship structured, and does commercetools see the SIs as primary delivery, primary innovation, or both?**
   - *Hypothesis being tested:* SIs are primary delivery + secondary co-innovation; ISVs handle primary innovation.
   - *What we learn:* how to position our product to be SI-friendly without competing for the SI's services revenue.

9. **The commercetools Solution Hub has a wide catalog of CDP, search, content, and personalization partners. How does commercetools decide which categories to deepen via marketplace partners vs which to bring native?**
   - *Hypothesis being tested:* deepen-via-partner is the default; native happens only when the category is core (catalog, cart, checkout, agentic discovery).
   - *What we learn:* whether storefront authoring + experimentation are deepen-via-partner (good for us) or native-bound (bad for us).

10. **Are there ISV partnership tiers — preferred / certified / partner-of-the-year — and what does it take to graduate between tiers?**
    - *Hypothesis being tested:* tiered relationship; the path runs through customer references + technical certification + co-marketing.
    - *What we learn:* the concrete steps to lock channel partnership status with commercetools itself, beyond SI partnerships.

## 4 · Competitive & M&A Frame

### Section purpose

Probe how commercetools sees the competitive landscape and what their M&A philosophy is. Test whether we are partnership-relevant or acquisition-relevant.

### Questions

11. **In the conversations you have with prospective customers, what's the most common reason they're considering commercetools over Adobe Commerce or Salesforce Commerce Cloud — and what's the most common reason they hesitate or stay with the suites?**
    - *Hypothesis being tested:* "agility / time-to-market / TCO" wins; "we already have Adobe / Salesforce relationships" hesitates.
    - *What we learn:* how commercetools' deals get won and lost — directly informs how we should pitch alongside them.

12. **commercetools has historically grown organically with selective acquisitions. What's the philosophy on M&A in 2026–2027 — focused on agentic commerce capabilities, geographic expansion, vertical depth, or something else?**
    - *Hypothesis being tested:* M&A philosophy is "partner deep, acquire rarely" — large acquisitions reserved for clearly strategic gaps (which the founder typically discloses).
    - *What we learn:* whether commercetools is in an active-buyer mode or a partner-everywhere mode.

13. **If you imagined a 5-year future where AI-native operator tools have completely re-shaped how businesses author and operate storefronts, what does commercetools' product surface look like — and what's *not* there because someone else owns it?**
    - *Hypothesis being tested:* commercetools sees its long-term position as the commerce backbone + agent discovery + checkout, with everything operator-facing delivered by SI/ISV ecosystem.
    - *What we learn:* the durable boundaries of commercetools' product scope. **High-leverage question — best signal for our long-term partnership/acquisition relevance.**

## 5 · Closing — Vision & Follow-Up

### Section purpose

End with an open-ended, forward-looking question that lets the CEO articulate vision, and leave a clear follow-up door open.

### Questions

14. **What would change your mind about your current AI / agentic-commerce strategy? What customer signal, competitive move, or technology shift would prompt a roadmap reorientation in the next 12 months?**
    - *Hypothesis being tested:* the answer surfaces the unstated risks and bets — useful for our own scenario planning.
    - *What we learn:* what to watch for as a signal that commercetools is reconsidering scope.

15. **Who at commercetools is the right person to continue this conversation on the operator-tooling and frontend layer specifically? Is there a product-side counterpart we should connect with?**
    - *Closing move:* sets up the CPO conversation as a natural follow-up; positions the relationship as multi-touchpoint.

### Optional ask (only if natural opening appears)

> *"As we develop this product, we'd value commercetools' input on integration patterns, marketplace fit, and where we should be careful not to step on roadmap items you're already pursuing. Would there be a structured way to share that intent — early-access partner program, technical advisory, joint-customer pilot? We're not asking for a commitment in this conversation; just curious whether such a path exists."*

## 6 · What to Listen For (Beyond the Answers)

| Signal | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| CEO is **enthusiastic** about Agentic Jumpstart, mentions it unprompted across multiple sections | Their AI bet is locked in at agent-discovery; storefront authoring is unlikely to be claimed back |
| CEO **hesitates or hedges** on the "where does AI investment stop" question | They have an internal debate about scope; our positioning may be less safe than the public roadmap suggests |
| CEO references **specific frontend / experience-layer competitors** (Frontastic, Builder.io, Vercel, Uniform) by name and with detailed framing | Frontend strategy is on their mind; the partnership gap may be filling internally |
| CEO **promotes the SI ecosystem repeatedly** and downplays direct ISV channel | SI-pitch motion is the right play; direct commercetools-channel ambition may be premature |
| CEO names **acquisition criteria** in any answer | M&A is closer than the public posture; tracking + lighthouse customer accumulation may matter materially |
| CEO **redirects to the CPO** for technical/product detail | Healthy delegation pattern; CPO conversation is where the product-strategy depth lives |
